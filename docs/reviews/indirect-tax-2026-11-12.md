# Review: indirect-tax.html, Explain (2026-11-12)
Spec: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-12-indirect-tax-explain-sugar-levy-design.md`   Reviewer: graph-reviewer   Verdict: **Rework** (by the rubric: one blocker. It is a rounding fix, and every major below has a clear fix.)

Scope: the Explain walkthrough (`#explain`), all 20 steps, at 1280×800 and at 390×844 with touch input. Play was checked only for Explain's two exits and for whether Play's chains reuse Explain's marks (P10). Things I noticed in Play outside that scope are listed at the end, not graded.

Screens: `docs/reviews/screens/indirect-tax-2026-11-12/` (shortened to `screens/` below).

## Summary
Mostly yes. A student who didn't get it in class gets the chain: S2 up by 24p is also a move left, the price rises by less than the tax (they guess first), Pp is read off S1 (they fit the wedge themselves), the box is 24p × 51m, and the box splits into two burdens. The tasks are real, they work by pointer, keyboard and touch, none can leave a student stuck, and every chain sentence in the spec can be said from the built page. What lets it down is mostly how it's drawn. At the busiest moments the S2 label is hidden behind the readout and the "S1 = what it costs to make" label sits right under the S2 line. The consumer burden is the exact same colour as the levy and the tax box. The price note says "Pe" while it climbs to 92p. On a phone, Q<sub>tax</sub> shrinks to 8px (4px on the last step). On the revenue step, the card's "height × width = revenue" doesn't multiply out on 14 of the 41 levy values the slider allows, including the 30p the probe asks about. That last one is the blocker.
