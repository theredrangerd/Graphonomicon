# IB Economics Interactive Graph Site — context for AI coding agents

Read this before making design or architectural decisions in this repo. The full design record, including rejected alternatives and rationale, lives in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-24-ib-econ-graph-site-design.md` — read that file too before resuming non-trivial design/implementation work; treat it as more authoritative than this summary if they ever diverge, and update both if a decision here changes.

## Mission and audience

This repo is expanding from a single prototype page (`equilibrium-lab.html`, being retired once its content is ported) into a full site of interactive, animated IB Economics graph explorers. The user is an IB Economics teacher building this primarily for their own G11 class, with informal sharing to other teachers/students as a bonus, not a requirement.

**The problem being solved:** hand-drawn graphs collapse into an illegible mess once a teacher annotates multiple relationships on top of the base curves (e.g. a price control, then consumer surplus, producer surplus, and deadweight loss, all in one color). **The bet:** interactivity (not animation alone) — a student dragging a control and watching shaded regions update live in distinct colors — is what makes cause (intervention) → effect (welfare change) click. This is **not** exam hand-drawing practice; it's for conceptual understanding.

**North Star: the animation is the chain of analysis.** Graphonomicon exists to make economics graphs *intuitive*, and the way it does that is by playing, in order, the cause → effect chain a student is expected to write about. When an intervention switches on, the diagram shows how theory gets from the cause to the outcome: for a subsidy, supply shifts, then the price each side faces moves (with the per-unit payment visible along the curve), then the surplus bands appear, then the numbers. A graph that jumps to the end state, or that animates only the *size* of the result (a box that simply grows), has missed the point even if it moves smoothly.
- **Order is logical, not temporal.** The sequence is the one an IB exam answer would give (comparative statics), not a simulation of prices adjusting over time. Never add dynamics the model doesn't claim. The ground truth for the order is the page's Explain steps.
- **Two tests.** (a) No mark appears before the thing that causes it. (b) Pause on any frame and a student could say it as one sentence of the chain.
- **Each lens is a stakeholder and answers one question: what is the impact on them?** (consumers: what do buyers gain? producers: what do sellers gain? government: what is the impact on its budget? society: where does the money go, and what is wasted?). IB evaluation turns on which stakeholder you're looking at, and the lens exists to teach that habit. Its animation replays the part of the Explain derivation that answers that question and nothing another lens already derives. Deadweight loss is society's, not the government's.
- **Play replays Explain's marks, compressed.** Reuse the visual vocabulary Explain established (e.g. the arrows down along S1) rather than inventing new ones, so the two modes read as one explanation.
- **The chain plays on toggle and lens switch only.** Slider drags go straight to the finished picture, and any interaction cancels a chain in progress. Worked examples: the Subsidy PS lens and the Societal lens.
- **Subsidy Play plays this for every lens (2026-11-12):** `subsidyChain(focus)` in `subsidy.js` is the chain per lens. All four open with one motion (the toggle's tween: arrows grow down from S1 while S2 and e2 slide down with them, nothing entering), then run their own part, drawn in (the arrows leave as it starts): CS the Pc line then the band; PS the arrow up to S1 and the Pc/Pp lines then the band; Government the Pc line, the arrow up to S1 and the Pp line, one strip per loaf, the spending box (no DWL); Societal Explain's box → mCS → mPS → "?" → DWL build-up. Switching off tweens the subsidy back out. The CS/PS/Government lenses have no note under the chart (only Societal narrates its stages). The lens buttons are one row of flat tiles (Government purple, Consumers blue, Producers orange, Society ink; each led by its colour square, the chosen one filled solid) with a status strip beneath it that always says what the graph shows: no lens = an ink "IB exam diagram" badge ("The whole picture. This is what to draw in an exam."), a lens = its question plus "Only part of the diagram. Exams need all of it." and a "Show the exam diagram" button back; the bare URL is the exam diagram, because students must never draw a lens (a fragment) as their exam answer with no "Pick something to highlight" prompt, and the chosen lens's stakeholder question (`SUBSIDY_QUESTIONS` in `viewSwitch.js`) shows above the graph; other pages keep the plain chips for now. **Known gap, left on purpose (owner, 2026-11-12):** the Market outcome tiles fill the moment the subsidy is on, ahead of the chain; to be reconsidered with the tiles themselves.

Two arrival patterns that must both work:
1. **Deep link to one specific graph** (dominant pattern) — a teacher shares a link to exactly one scenario, in-class or for revision.
2. **Cold browse/revision** — a student who doesn't know the taxonomy uses homepage search or unit/family browsing.

This split is *why* the architecture below looks the way it does — don't propose a "unified mega-page with toggles" design; it was explicitly considered and rejected (see spec, "Information architecture: engine vs. pages").

## Architecture: shared engine + focused pages

- **One shared rendering engine** (curves, shading, labels, animation) built once as a plain JS module, imported by every graph page.
- **Separate, focused page per teaching scenario** (Price Ceiling, Price Floor, Tax, Subsidy, Labor Market, etc.), each independently deep-linkable, each showing only the controls relevant to that scenario.
- Pages within a topic are cross-linked as a "family" so a student can hop Price Ceiling → Price Floor → Tax without losing context.
- **Rule for toggle vs. separate page:** if two variants share axes/curve shapes and differ only by parameter (specific vs. ad valorem tax; externality before/after Pigouvian tax; regulated vs. unregulated natural monopoly) → one page, mode toggle. If curve/region shapes genuinely differ (price ceiling vs. price floor; perfect competition vs. monopoly) → separate pages. Apply case-by-case as each family is built, not as an exhaustive upfront classification.

## Site structure and routing

- Multi-page mode, no client-side router: every page is a real `.html` file (`index.html`, `units/microeconomics.html`, `units/microeconomics/<family>.html`, `units/microeconomics/<family>/<diagram>.html`, ...). This is deliberate — it suits GitHub Pages hosting and keeps deep-linking trivial.
- Nesting for a unit with many diagrams (Microeconomics has ~40): unit page (one tile per topic family) → family page (one tile per diagram) → diagram page (the actual interactive graph, and the deep-link target). Search on the homepage resolves directly to a diagram page regardless of nesting depth, so the extra navigation layer doesn't hurt the "share one link" use case.
- **Three-tier WIP fallback:** any tile — unit, family, or diagram — that isn't built yet links to the shared generic "Work in Progress" stub page (`units/wip.html`, driven by `src/pages/wip.js`), which embeds a Google Form for feedback. Never leave a dead link or silently omit an unbuilt item; always route through the WIP stub. The WIP stub is intentionally generic/shared, not per-unit.
- Homepage unit tiles use a **bento grid with fixed, tiered sizes** — Microeconomics = large, Macroeconomics = medium, International = small, Development = small — configured as a static value, not computed from live graph count. Do not "fix" this to be computed dynamically; that was explicitly rejected (it would make small-but-complete units shrink to unclickable slivers and cause layout reflow on every release). If the assumed content-volume ratio ever proves wrong, it's a one-line config change, not a redesign.
- Coming-soon units/families stay **visible** on the site (not hidden), consistent with the principle that the site should be honest about current completeness.

## Codebase layout

- `index.html` — homepage entry.
- `units/wip.html` — generic Work-in-Progress stub page (embeds the feedback Google Form).
- `src/pages/` — per-page logic (`home.js` + `home-entry.js`, `wip.js` + `wip-entry.js`); `*-entry.js` files are the Vite build entry points, `*.js` (no `-entry`) hold the testable logic.
- `src/components/` — shared UI pieces: `chrome.js` (injects shared header/nav/hero markup into every page at load time), `bento.js` (the homepage unit grid), `search.js` (homepage search).
- `src/lib/units.js` — unit/family/diagram data model (syllabus taxonomy, tier config, build status).
- `src/lib/explainRuntime.js` — what every Explain walkthrough shares beyond the step card (`explainCard.js`): its curves (`explainMarket`, `EXPLAIN_DEMAND`), the demand-curve tween (`demandTween`), the slider-fits check and `keepStepCardInView`. A new Explain page uses these rather than copying them; its `follow(step, animate, draw)` callback must only draw, never call back into `follow`.
- `src/styles/tokens.css` — shared design tokens (colors/fonts), carried over from `equilibrium-lab.html`'s original `:root` variables; `src/styles/pages.css` — shared page-level styles.
- `vite.config.js` — multi-page build config; each real page must be registered under `build.rollupOptions.input` (see `main`/`wip` entries) or Vite won't emit it.
- Tests are colocated (`*.test.js` next to the file it tests) using Vitest + jsdom; run with `npm run test`.

## Per-unit visual identity

Each syllabus unit gets an accent color token (e.g. `--unit-microeconomics: var(--demand)` for Microeconomics, currently blue) used on its homepage tile *and* carried through as the accent (header underline, hairlines, hover/active states — not a full background wash) on that unit's pages and family sub-pages. When adding a new unit or family page, apply the existing unit accent token rather than introducing new ad hoc unit colors.

## Definition of "done" for a new graph page

- Interactivity matches the price-ceiling/DWL quality bar: moving a control live-updates the relevant regions (surplus, DWL, tax revenue, etc.) in distinct colors — never a static annotated image.
- **Any overlaid policy intervention gets an explicit on/off toggle, defaulting to off.** Price ceiling, price floor, indirect tax, and subsidy all use the same `.switch` control to impose/remove the intervention on top of the free-market baseline — this is a rule for every future graph, not a per-page choice. Parameter sliders (ceiling price, tax rate, subsidy amount, ...) stay visible and live regardless of toggle state; only whether the intervention reaches `computeMarket` (vs. `{ type: 'none' }`) depends on the toggle. This does *not* apply to mode toggles with no "off" state (e.g. specific vs. ad valorem once a tax is already on) — those stay as radio-button mode switches. See spec doc, "Intervention on/off toggle" for full rationale.
- **The intervention toggle plays the chain, not just a fade.** Sliders already read as animated since dragging fires continuous `input` events; a toggle's `change` event fires once, so without help the whole diagram would snap. On every future intervention page, wire the toggle's `change` handler through `tweenValue` (`src/lib/animate.js`) to ramp the intervention from off to its slider value (or back) over ~400ms, re-rendering the chart every frame — never call `render()` directly from the toggle handler. But tweening the magnitude is the *minimum*, not the goal: where the page has a chain of analysis (see the North Star in Mission), the toggle plays it in order, as the Subsidy lenses' staged reveals do, rather than letting every region scale up together. For a magnitude-style intervention (tax, subsidy) tween the amount/rate from 0 to the slider value; for a level-style intervention (ceiling, floor) tween the displayed price from the free-market `Pstar` (computed via `computeMarket({..., intervention: { type: 'none' }})`) to the slider price — either way the animation must drive the same `computeMarket` call the page already uses, so stats, fills, and DWL animate in sync with the curve instead of just the line moving. `tweenValue` no-ops to the end value under Vitest (`import.meta.env.MODE === 'test'`) so page-controller tests stay synchronous — don't add per-test workarounds for this instead.
- SL/HL tag visible on both the diagram page and its tile.
- Linked from its family page and indexed by homepage search.
- Embeds the site's feedback form per the existing pattern.
- **Header nav is a single `#mini-header` row, never a second nav element.** Wire it via `getFamilyNav(currentSlug, { familySlug })` (in `familyNav.js`) → `renderMiniHeader({ title, backHref, backLabel, siblings })` (in `chrome.js`). This renders, in order: back-to-family link → home link → current title → sibling pill-chip links to every *other* diagram in the family. Don't hand-roll a separate sibling-nav container per page — an earlier version did this and produced a duplicate, redundant back link. See spec doc, "Diagram-page header nav" for the full rationale and the `.mini-header__siblings` pill styling (uses the generic `--accent`/`--accent-fill` token pair, not a hardcoded color).
- **Two-column layout: `.main-col` (left) holds the chart card + "Market outcome" panel stacked together; `.sidebar` (right) holds only controls** — intervention panel first (toggle + its own parameters), then "Shift the curves" (demand/supply position sliders), then "Elasticity" (slope sliders only). Results live on the left so a student never looks away from the chart+numbers to read an outcome; controls live on the right, kept visually compact (`.sidebar .panel`/`.sidebar .slider-row` use tighter spacing than the general `.panel`/`.slider-row` rules). No reset-to-defaults button — it was removed sitewide along with the `DEFAULTS` JS object that backed it; don't add one back without a concrete reason. See spec doc, "Two-column layout: controls column vs. results column".
- **The two columns must end at the same bottom edge — an exact match, not "close enough."** Achieved with pure CSS: `.layout` has no `align-items` override (grid's default `stretch` makes both columns match the taller one's height), and `.main-col > *:last-child` / `.sidebar > *:last-child` get `flex: 1` so the last panel on the shorter side (usually the sidebar's "Real world examples") grows to absorb the difference — never leave dead whitespace below a normal-height card instead. Turned off below the `880px` mobile breakpoint, where columns stack into separate rows. See spec doc, "Matched column height".
- **The intervention toggle is visually the star of the page**, not a plain checkbox: apply the `.toggle-row--primary` modifier class to the "Impose ___" row (bigger switch, bolder label, tinted/bordered callout box) on every future intervention panel. Every other toggle-row (mode-select radios, etc.) stays default size. See spec doc, "Intervention on/off toggle".
- **Last sidebar panel is "Real world examples":** a `.examples-list` of ~3 links to real, freely-readable articles/references giving concrete real-world instances of the policy the page models. **Source these via an actual web search and only use URLs the search results return — never construct or guess a URL**, even a plausible-looking one (this is a hard rule, not specific to this feature — see the top-level "never guess URLs" instruction). Prefer free sources (major news orgs, public media, nonprofit data journalism, or Wikipedia for a canonical case). Links open in a new tab with `rel="noopener noreferrer"`. **Hard rule: at least one of the examples must be non-Western** (its subject matter — the country/region the policy happened in — outside Europe and North America; e.g. India, the Philippines, Indonesia, Argentina all count), specifically to counter the Western-media default skew that unguided searches produce. Check this explicitly before finalizing the 3 links; don't assume it happens by accident. See spec doc, "Sidebar panel order and content" for full rationale and current examples.
- **The 3 example cards must visually fill the panel and use categorical color, matching Market outcome's stat tiles** — `.examples-panel`/`.examples-list`/its `li`s use `flex: 1` so the cards stretch to fill whatever height the matched-column-height rule gives the panel (no dead whitespace under fixed-size cards), and each card cycles through the same `--demand`/`--supply`/`--gov` fill+border tokens (via `li:nth-child(3n+1/2/3)`) that Market outcome's `.stat.cs`/`.stat.ps`/`.stat.gov` already use — reuse the palette, don't invent a new one. **The purple/`--gov` variant is intentionally toned down** (`color-mix` at 8%/22%/65% instead of the full-strength token/fill) since violet reads louder than blue/orange at equal opacity — don't restore it to full strength.
- **No explanatory hint text under the elasticity sliders** (no `.slider-hint` paragraphs like "Flatter = buyers react strongly..."). The numeric elasticity readout (e.g. "1.33 · elastic") is fine since it's feedback from the student's own action — and it must be *true point elasticity at equilibrium* (`pointElasticities()`), never a word derived from slope. The curves default to **unit elastic at equilibrium** (`UNIT_ELASTIC_SLOPE` in `pageControls.js`) as the SL-friendly baseline, so the readouts start at "1.00 · unit elastic" and still tell the truth. **Allowed exception:** the one-line `.panel-disclaimer` under the Elasticity heading ("Simplified: … shown here at the equilibrium. Why? →", linking via `elasticityInfoHref()` to the Elasticity family / WIP stub) is required on every page with elasticity sliders. It flags a simplification rather than explaining the slider, so it isn't a banned hint. Don't remove it, and don't add further hint text; a written explanation defeats the "learn by playing" goal — a student should discover what a flatter/steeper curve means by dragging the slider, not read it first. This applies to every future graph page with elasticity controls, not just the four existing Government Intervention pages.
- **Curve sliders follow the slider model in the spec ("Slider model", 2026-09-29):** shift sliders are signed offsets where right = more demand/supply; elasticity sliders are right = flatter, pivoting through the equilibrium; the engine never clamps to the chart, and `guardSliders()`/`fitsChart()` stop sliders at the chart edge instead. Wire new pages through `readCurveParams()` + `wireShiftAndSlopeInputs(doc, render, isValid)` rather than reading raw slider values. Before shipping any new graph page, check it against the spec's "Known failure modes" list.
- **Chart marks follow the spec's "Chart marking conventions" (2026-09-29):** IB axis symbols (Pe/Qe always, plus P<sub>max</sub>/P<sub>min</sub>/Qs/Qd or Pc/Pp with Q<sub>tax</sub>/Q<sub>subsidy</sub>). Shortage/excess supply is drawn as a labelled bracket, and a floor's gap is always called "excess supply", never "surplus". Lines are labelled as prices and bands as burden/benefit. A market closed by the intervention reports "—", not made-up prices. Equilibrium points are labelled e<sub>1</sub> (always) and e<sub>2</sub> (only when the intervention moves the equilibrium: tax/subsidy, never ceiling/floor), at every detail level.
- **Hovering a shaded area raises its fill opacity; nothing changes size** (user's call, 2026-10-29). `pages.css` mixes each fill token toward its solid colour on `.region-hover`/`.region-active`. There's no scale, no brightness filter, and no CS/PS pop-over on the subsidy overlap stripes. Hovering the free-market CS/PS triangle also lights up its marginal CS/PS band. Any new fill class needs a matching hover rule there.
- **Chart text is real-pixel sized:** `marketChart.js` sizes its viewBox to the rendered width (1 unit = 1 CSS px) and redraws on resize, with a fixed type scale (13px ticks/labels, 14px curve names, 16px axis symbols, all `--ink-secondary` or darker). Never go back to a fixed viewBox that scales text down; give any new chart label its own class instead of reusing `.tick-label`. See spec, "Chart marking conventions".
- **No narration for the untouched free-market baseline in `#market-note`.** It used to read "Equilibrium price and quantity — every mutually beneficial trade happens." — removed, since the baseline is the state a student starts from and needs no caption. `.note:empty` now hides itself so this doesn't leave a blank pill. This does *not* extend to the other note states (shortage/surplus amounts, DWL/welfare-loss figures, "not binding") — those report a computed, state-dependent result and were intentionally left in place; only the "nothing has happened yet" narration was cut.
- **Every graph page gets the detail gear + first-visit walkthrough** via `initGraphChrome(doc, { chartHead, steps, onDetailChange })` (`src/components/graphChrome.js`), created *before* the first render; pass `detail: chrome.getDetail()` to the chart renderer on every render and call `maybeAutoStartTour(chrome)` after the first one. Detail levels are `full` (today's chart — the setting only removes, never adds), `clean` (no chips of any kind — region chips *and* number annotations like the subsidy per-unit amount chip/bracket; only axis tick values remain) and `ib` (exactly an IB exam diagram: plain area names, no chips, no numbers); tapping a shaded area opens its explainer at **every** level; any new renderer must honour that contract. **Known failure mode:** a computed-number annotation (per-unit amount, rate, revenue figure…) must be gated `detail === 'full'`, never `!== 'ib'` — the subsidy amount chip once leaked into Clean that way. Each page supplies its own tour steps (`interventionTourSteps()` for intervention pages); tour copy says what a student can *do*, never what the graph teaches. **Tour spotlights must cover exactly what the step text names** — step 2 ("these sliders") targets only the sidebar panels that contain a range input (`.sidebar .panel:has(input[type="range"])`), never the whole `.sidebar`, which also holds the non-slider "Real world examples" panel; a step selector may match several elements and the spotlight wraps their combined box. Check every new page's tour targets against their wording (known failure mode, spec: onboarding doc, "Walkthrough"). The tour runs once per browser site-wide; the replay button is fixed bottom-left. See `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-25-graph-onboarding-and-detail-settings-design.md`.
- **Subsidy and Indirect tax use Explain/Play (exception to the detail-gear rule above).** `subsidy.html` has `#explain` (guided 14-step walkthrough: `explainCard.js` + `subsidyExplainSteps.js`; drag a dot along S1 (Next locked until explored) → why S2 is lower → two prices → the box as government spending, built strip by strip → where that money goes; spec `2026-11-09-subsidy-explain-pedagogy-design.md`) and `#play` (also the bare URL), switched by `modeSwitch.js`; mode/view/focus URL state (`?view=explorer&focus=gov|cs|ps`) lives in `src/lib/pageMode.js`, never localStorage. **Subsidy Explorer mirrors the tax page's chips (2026-11-12): Government perspective (`gov`: the spending box only, the impact on its budget; deadweight loss lives in Societal) | Consumer lens (`cs`: old CS + mCS band) | Producer lens (`ps`: old PS + mPS band) | Societal lens (`societal`, 2026-11-12: just mCS, mPS and the DWL inside the spending box's outline; on switching the subsidy on it plays Explain's build-up as `SOCIETAL_STAGES` in `subsidy.js` — strips fill the box, box dissolves into mCS, then mPS, then the grey "?" corner, then DWL, then the chips); Overlap and the standalone DWL focus were dropped, and old `?focus=dwl` links open `societal` and `?focus=overlap` opens `cs` via `SUBSIDY_FOCUS_ALIASES`. In a lens the free-market triangle's chip is "old CS/PS" (triangle area only), never the total, so it can't be read as including the marginal band.** Play calls `initGraphChrome(..., { detailSettings: false })` — **no detail gear**; an IB perfect | Explorer switch (`viewSwitch.js`) + focus bar replaces it, and the tour's last step targets `.view-switch` (`interventionTourSteps({ viewSwitch: true })`; tour auto-runs only in Play). `SUBSIDY_AREA_LABELS` ('full'|'half'|'none') in `subsidy.js` is the contingency for Ms. Patel's IB-label question. `marketChart.js` now draws by named layers (`LAYERS`, `resolveLayers`; options `layers`, `captions`, `enter`, `grow`, `focus`, `areaLabels`): **any new drawn element must belong to a layer.** The family page splits the Subsidy tile into Explain / Play (`tileSplit.js`, `diagramModeLinks`). Other pages keep the gear until ported. Spec: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-27-subsidy-explain-and-play-design.md`. **Indirect tax now has the same Explain/Play treatment (2026-11-09):** `indirect-tax.js` + `indirectTaxExplainSteps.js` (12 steps, soft-drink sugar tax, specific tax only, step 11 tweens to inelastic demand); Explorer foci cs | ps | consumer-burden | producer-burden | revenue | dwl (`TAX_FOCUSES`; a CS/PS focus never draws a burden band, and burdens are filled in their own violet `--burden-consumer`/`--burden-producer` tokens, never teal/gold, so they can't read as a kind of surplus in `viewSwitch.js`; pages pass their own focus list to `readPlayView`); `TAX_AREA_LABELS` is the tax's own label setting; the axis reads Qe / Q<sub>tax</sub>. Decisions: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-09-indirect-tax-explain-and-play-design.md`. **Price ceiling/floor now have Explore too (2026-11-11; no Explain yet):** `price-control.js` mounts the same IB perfect | Explorer switch + focus bar (`CEILING_FOCUSES`/`FLOOR_FOCUSES`: cs | ps | gap | dwl; `gap` = the shortage / excess-supply bracket) and no detail gear. **Standard for every gap bracket: it hangs below the x-axis, spanning Qs to Qd under their axis symbols, label beneath it — never on the diagram.** Pages pass `reserveGapBand: true` so the plot doesn't resize when the control toggles.
